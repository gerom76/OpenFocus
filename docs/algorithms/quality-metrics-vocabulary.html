Quality metrics: what each number
actually measures

Every fusion and registration algorithm in OpenFocus is tuned against the same set of numbers. This sheet says, in plain English, what each of those numbers means, which direction is better, and — the part that matters most — what each one cannot see. No metric here is a verdict on its own. They are used together precisely because each is blind to something another one catches.

The organising idea is what a metric needs as input. That decides where you are allowed to use it. A metric that needs a perfect answer to compare against can only run on synthetic test scenes; a metric that needs nothing but the result can run on any photograph you take.

The five families

  • Full-reference — needs the perfect answer. Only exists on synthetic scenes. PSNR, SSIM, region PSNR, colour error.
  • No-reference — needs the result and the source frames. Works on any real stack. QABF, spatial frequency, entropy, std dev.
  • Artefact — needs the result alone. Hunts one specific defect. Seam excess/visible, defocus seams, block speckle, envelope.
  • Foreign-reference — needs another program's render of the same capture. Not a truth, just a second opinion. Detail agreement, detail share.
  • Geometric — needs the true per-frame transform. Registration only, and it is exact. Residual, gain, kept, selection share.

What each family can and cannot be run on

Read left to right and the trade-off is visible: the further right you go, the weaker the evidence, but the more real the picture it was measured on. Full-reference metrics are the most trustworthy numbers OpenFocus has and also the least representative — a rendered test scene is not a photograph of an insect. That is why nothing is decided on one family alone.

Where the numbers come fromthe seven test scenarios
Scenariosdeep stack · depth edge · fine texture · long stack · low contrast · saturated colour · sensor noise. Each isolates one thing a fusion method can get wrong.
Why sevenA method that wins on texture and collapses on a depth edge has not won. Every observed range on this sheet spans all seven, which is why the spreads look wide.
The handheld sample sceneswhere registration is measured
What they holdThe exact transform applied to every frame, recorded when the scene was built. Alignment error is therefore computed, not judged.
ConsequenceRegistration is the one part of the pipeline whose quality is known exactly. The numbers on page 6 are arithmetic; every other page is inference.
The regression baselinehow a change is judged
How it worksEvery method's score on every scenario is recorded. A change that moves any of them by more than its tolerance has to be explained before it is accepted.
ToleranceListed per metric on page 7. It absorbs ordinary run-to-run variation — a move inside it is not a small win, it is no evidence at all.

Family 1 · needs the perfect answer

Full-reference metrics

Synthetic scenes only — a real focus stack has no all-in-focus truth to compare against
PSNRPeak signal-to-noise ratio
higher betterdB
The average pixel error, rewritten as a number that grows as the error shrinks. Take how far every pixel is from where it should be, square it, average it, and put it on a logarithmic scale. Adding 6 dB means the error was cut in half.
Goes upThe result matches the truth pixel for pixel. Infinite on an exact match.
Goes downAny deviation at all — blur, noise, a shifted edge, a colour cast.
Blind toWhere the error is. A faint softness spread over the whole frame and one ugly tear in the subject's eye can score identically. It has no idea what a picture is.
Watch outNeeds pixel-perfect alignment. Shift the result one pixel and PSNR collapses, even though nothing looks worse. That is why it is useless against another program's render.
Observed across 6 fusion methods × 7 test scenarios
23.2 dB — worstmedian 35.969.4 dB — best
SSIMStructural similarity index
higher better−1 … 1
Slides a small soft window over both images and, at every position, asks whether the local brightness, the local contrast and the local pattern agree. It asks “does this look like the same thing?” where PSNR asks “is this the same number?”.
Goes upStructure is preserved — edges land in the same place with the same contrast. 1.0 is identical.
Goes downBlur, ringing, halos, smearing — anything that changes local structure rather than overall level.
Blind toIts own top end. Real results crowd into 0.97–1.00, so the difference between “good” and “excellent” is buried in the third decimal. Read the change, not the value.
Use it forCoherence. When a change makes the result hang together better without moving pixel error much, SSIM sees it and PSNR mostly does not.
Observed across 6 fusion methods × 7 test scenarios
0.894 — worstmedian 0.9941.000 — best
Region PSNRPSNR inside a boundary band
higher betterdB
The same PSNR, but computed only in a narrow strip — six pixels or so — hugging the borders where one depth meets another. That strip is where fusion has to make its hardest choice, and it is where halos and bleeding live.
Why separateThe boundary is a tiny share of the frame. Averaged into a whole-frame score, a bad halo around every leg vanishes into a rounding error.
Goes downBright fringes beside a dark subject, background bleeding into the subject, an edge taken from the wrong frame.
Read withWhole-frame PSNR. Interior high and boundary low is the signature of a method that reconstructs flat areas well and mishandles depth edges.
NeedsThe per-frame focus masks, so it is a synthetic-scene measurement.
Interpretation
boundary far below interior = halosequal = clean edges
Colour errorMean absolute channel deviation
lower betterlevels, 0–255
How far off the colour is, in the units your histogram is drawn in. Compare red with red, green with green, blue with blue, take the average distance. A value of 3 means the typical channel is three levels out of 255 away from correct.
Why it existsPSNR and SSIM are computed on brightness. Hues can drift a long way while both still look healthy — this is what “the colours came out wrong” measures as.
Goes upA fusion rule applied per channel that picks different frames for red and blue, or tone shifts from blending.
Blind toStructure entirely. A perfectly graded but completely blurred result scores well here.
Rule of thumbUnder 1 level is invisible. Above 3–4 is a visible cast on smooth gradients.
Visibility
0 — exact~3 — starts to show10+ — obvious cast
Ground truththe thing being compared against
The image the result should have been — every part of the subject sharp, and known to be correct because the scene was built rather than photographed.
Where fromSynthetic stacks: a sharp scene is rendered, then blurred by depth to manufacture the frames a camera would have taken. The unblurred original is the truth.
The catchA real focus stack has no such image. If it existed there would be nothing to fuse. That single fact is why the rest of this sheet exists.
SoEverything on this page is a laboratory measurement — precise, trustworthy, and taken on a scene that is not a photograph of an insect.
Reading decibelsthe dB scale, for PSNR
A dB is not a linear unit. Each step of about 6 dB means the error was halved. That is why 30 dB and 36 dB are not “close together”.
Rough guideUnder 25 dB: visibly wrong. 30–35 dB: acceptable. Over 40 dB: differences are hard to see. Over 60 dB: essentially identical, and usually a flat, easy scene.
Why 69 dB appearsThe low-contrast test scenario has almost nothing to get wrong. A spectacular PSNR there says the scene was easy, not that the method is good.
CompareOnly differences within one scenario. A 0.75 dB move is the point at which the regression suite treats a change as real.

Family 2 · needs only the result and the source frames

No-reference metrics

The set that applies to real focus stacks, where no correct answer exists to compare against
QABFQAB/F — gradient fusion quality
(Xydeas & Petrovic, 2000)
higher better0 … 1
Of all the edge detail that was present somewhere in the source frames, what fraction survived into the fused result? Strong edges count for more than faint ones, and an edge only counts if it kept both its strength and its direction.
Why it leadsIt needs no ground truth, so it is the headline number for real stacks — the one quoted when comparing methods on an actual capture.
Goes upThe method picked the sharp frame everywhere and did not soften the edge on its way through.
Goes downDetail lost to blending, edges softened by smoothing, regions sourced from a defocused frame.
Blind toColour, tone, and seams. It also cannot tell a correct edge from a consistent one: an edge faithfully copied from the wrong frame still scores.
Observed across 6 fusion methods × 7 test scenarios
0.336 — worstmedian 0.6120.846 — best
Spatial frequencyRMS of the row and column gradients
higher usually betterlevels
How busy the picture is. Measure how much brightness changes from each pixel to its neighbour going across, and going down, and combine the two. A blurred image barely changes between neighbours and scores low; a crisp one scores high.
Goes upRetained fine detail and crisp edges.
Also goes upSensor noise, block seams, speckle, ringing. Every defect that adds contrast between neighbouring pixels raises this score.
SoNever read it alone. Rising spatial frequency is good news only when the seam and speckle numbers on page 4 held still. A noisy render is the easiest way to top this metric.
ScaleDepends entirely on the subject. Compare methods on one scene, never one method across scenes.
Observed across 6 fusion methods × 7 test scenarios — note the 37× spread, it is subject-driven
2.4 — flat scenemedian 29.890.7 — fine texture
EntropyShannon entropy of the histogram
higher usually betterbits, 0 … 8
How many different brightness values the picture actually uses, and how evenly. A flat grey card uses one value and carries almost no information: near 0 bits. A rich photograph spreads across the whole range: near 8 bits, the most an 8-bit image can hold.
Goes upMore tonal information preserved — detail that would otherwise be crushed into a single level survives.
Also goes upNoise, which is the purest form of “many different values”. Entropy cannot distinguish information from randomness.
Blind toArrangement, completely. Shuffle every pixel in the frame and entropy does not move at all.
Use it asA supporting number: a drop in entropy alongside a drop in QABF confirms detail was genuinely lost rather than merely relocated.
Scale
0 — one flat tone~7.5 typical photo8 — theoretical max
Std devGrayscale standard deviation
higher usually betterlevels
How far a typical pixel sits from the average brightness of the frame — a one-number stand-in for contrast. A washed-out result huddles around mid-grey and scores low; one with deep blacks and clean highlights scores high.
Goes downAveraging across frames, which is what blending does when it cannot decide. This is the clearest symptom of a fusion rule that hedges.
Blind toWhere the contrast came from. Clipping the blacks raises it. So does adding noise. So does a seam.
Read asA sanity check, not a target. A large unexplained drop against the source frames means the method is washing the picture out. It costs nothing to compute, which is why it is logged everywhere — but it is the weakest evidence on this page.
Interpretation
below the source frames = washed outmatching = contrast preserved
The noise trapread this before quoting page 3
Three of the four metrics on this page — spatial frequency, entropy and std dev — go up when you add noise to the picture. All three would call a grainy render an improvement.
WhyEach is measuring “variation”. Detail is variation. So is noise. Nothing in the arithmetic separates them.
The failureTune a method to maximise spatial frequency and you will end up with a sharpened, noisy result that scores beautifully and prints badly.
The defenceQABF is the exception: noise in the result matches no edge in any source frame, so it does not earn credit. Lead with QABF; use the other three to confirm, never to decide.
What “no reference” coststhe limit of this whole family
These metrics can tell you detail survived. None of them can tell you it ended up in the right place.
The blind spotTake a sharp region from frame 12 and paste it where frame 30 should have won. The edges are all present and all crisp: QABF is content.
Caught byThe artefact metrics on page 4, which know where a wrong choice has to leave its mark, and the geometric checks on page 6.
Still, it leadsThis is the only family that runs on the stacks people actually shoot. It is the headline evidence because it is the available evidence.

Family 3 · needs the result alone

Artefact metrics — each hunts one specific defect

General metrics average defects away; these look exactly where a known defect has to live
Seam excessblock_seams — part 1
lower better
How big a brightness step sits on the block grid, beyond the step you would expect from the picture there. In levels. Zero means the grid is invisible.
The ideaReal detail does not know where a block-based method's lattice is. Only a fusion mistake lines up exactly with it.
Applies toBlock-selection methods — DCT above all, where the block size is a tuning parameter this number responds to directly.
Blind toRarity. A hundred tiny steps and one enormous tear give the same mean — which is why the metric next to it exists.
Observed, all methods × scenarios
0.003med 5.221.4
Seam visibleblock_seams — part 2
lower better%
What percentage of the block grid actually shows — the share of boundary pixels whose step is more than three times the local norm.
Why pairedExcess says how bad, visible says how much. One 100-level tear is a defect anyone notices; a hundred 1-level steps is not. A mean alone cannot tell them apart.
Goes upThe lattice is showing across large parts of the frame.
Read asLow excess + low visible = clean. High excess + low visible = one bad tear. Low excess + high visible = a faint grid everywhere.
Regression tolerance: 2.0 percentage points
0 % — invisible30 %+ — grid on show
Defocus seamsdefocus_seams — excess & visible
lower better
The same two numbers, measured only in the parts of the picture that have no detail — the smooth, defocused background.
Why restrictA seam is most objectionable exactly where there is nothing to hide behind. A method can score respectably overall while tearing the background to pieces.
Self-locatingThe quiet region is found from the fused image itself, so this needs no reference and runs on any real stack.
Sharpest testOf the artefact set, this is the one that most reliably matches what a viewer complains about.
Observed excess, all methods × scenarios
0.0med 0.3225.7
Block speckleblock_speckle
lower better%
The percentage of flat-area blocks standing far outside their neighbours — lone squares that came from the wrong frame.
The defectA dust speck that happens to be sharp in exactly one frame wins its block. One square, wildly out of place, immediately obvious to a viewer.
Why neededThe seam metrics average along the grid — the wrong shape for an isolated square, which contributes almost nothing to a mean.
Earned its placeIt was added after a mean-based seam score ranked a visibly speckled render the best of a parameter sweep.
Observed; tolerance 0.15 pp
0.0 %med 0.166.25 %
Envelope excursionstack envelope check
must be zerolevels
How far the result strayed outside the darkest and brightest value any source frame had at that pixel. Anything above zero is a value the fusion invented.
Plain readingIf no frame ever recorded a pixel brighter than 180 there, a result of 205 did not come from the scene. It came from the algorithm.
Symptom ofRinging and halos — the overshoot that transform-based methods produce beside a strong edge.
Hard gateIn autotuning this is not scored, it is a veto. A candidate that leaves the envelope is rejected however well it scores on everything else.
Pass condition
(0, 0) — the only passing value
Why this family exists
A general metric asks “is the picture good?” and answers by averaging over millions of pixels. A defect that occupies 0.2 % of the frame and ruins the print is, to an average, noise.
The patternEach metric here encodes a hypothesis about where a specific algorithm puts its mistakes — on the block grid, in the flat background, outside the source range — and then looks only there.
ConsequenceThey are not comparable between algorithm types. Seam metrics on a pyramid method measure nothing, because a pyramid has no lattice to tear along.
DirectionAll of them: lower is better, zero is the goal. Unlike everything on pages 2 and 3, there is no trade-off — nothing is lost by driving these to zero.

Family 4 · needs another program's render of the same capture

Foreign-reference metrics

A second opinion, not a ground truth — and it is neither aligned to our result nor graded like it
Tone matchingmatch_tone — a preparation step,
not a score
no direction
Before comparing anything, regrade one image onto the other's tone by matching their histograms, channel by channel. Another program's render of the same capture carries its own exposure, its own contrast curve, its own colour grade.
The problemNone of that grading is a property of the fusion — but every intensity metric reads it as error, and a very large one. Uncorrected, the comparison measures taste, not quality.
What it removesExactly the class of difference a global brightness curve can express, and nothing more. Where the detail sits cannot survive a per-channel lookup table, so real differences pass through untouched.
AppliedPer candidate, so a single source frame is regraded onto its own tone the same way the fused result is — neither is charged for a grade it never chose.
Limit8-bit images only, which is the scale the metrics here are defined on.
Detail mapdetail_map — the shared building block
an image, not a number
A small picture of where the detail is: divide the frame into 64 × 64 pixel tiles and record the average local contrast in each. A photograph of a beetle becomes a coarse map with bright tiles on the resolved parts and dark tiles on the blur.
Why tilesTwo programs' renders of one capture sit a few pixels apart — each aligned its own way, and focus breathing means no single warp relates them.
The consequenceA pixel-by-pixel comparison would mostly measure that offset. A tile several times wider than the offset does not notice it.
This is whyPSNR cannot be used against a foreign render at all, and the two metrics below can.
Tile size trade-off
smaller = sharper, but sees misalignment64 pxlarger = blunter
Detail agreementdetail_agreement — first return value
higher better−1 … 1
How well the two detail maps correlate: do we and the other program agree about which parts of the frame resolve? At 1.0 both found detail in all the same places. At 0 the two disagree completely about what is sharp.
Goes downWe resolved a region they left blurred, or — the worrying direction — they resolved something we did not.
Blind toAmount. A heavily blurred copy of the reference keeps decent agreement while recovering no detail at all: the pattern of where detail would be is unchanged.
HenceNever quoted without the share, next card. Either one alone is trivially easy to satisfy.
Scale
0 — no relationship~0.85 healthy1.0
Detail sharedetail_agreement — second return value
1.0 is the targetratio
How much local contrast we recovered, as a fraction of theirs. 1.0 means as much detail as the other program pulled out of the same frames.
Below 1Softer. Detail the capture contained is being lost somewhere in our pipeline.
Above 1Either genuinely better recovery or amplified noise and sharpening artefacts. An image of pure noise agrees with nothing yet scores an enormous share.
Read the pairHigh agreement and share near 1.0 is the only combination that means what it appears to mean.
Interpretation
0.7 softer1.0 matched1.3 crunchier — check for noise
Why PSNR is banned herethe two differences that are not errors
Two programs given the same capture return two images that differ in ways no metric should be counting — and that a pixel-comparison counts most heavily of all.
Difference 1Grade. Their exposure, contrast curve and colour rendering are choices, not quality. Removed by tone matching.
Difference 2Position. Each program aligned the stack its own way, and focus breathing means no single warp relates the two results. Removed by pooling into tiles.
Left overWhere the detail is, and how much of it there is. Those two survive both corrections — which is exactly why they are the two numbers reported.
The reference is a peerhow much weight to give it
Another program's render is one more attempt at the same difficult problem. It is evidence, and it is the only evidence available on a real capture that comes from outside our own code — but it is not an answer key.
Disagreement meansOne of us handled that region better. Which one is a question to open, by looking at the region, not a verdict to record.
Both can be wrongNothing here detects a defect the two programs share. Two renders agreeing is not truth; it is a coincidence of approach.
Its real valueIt catches the failures our own metrics are structurally blind to, because it was not designed around our assumptions.

Family 5 · needs the true per-frame transform

Registration metrics — measured in pixels, not impressions

The handheld sample scenes record the exact shift applied to every frame, so alignment error is known, not inferred
Residual — meanmean px
lower betterpx
After alignment, how far a point of the scene still lands from where the reference frame put it — averaged over the whole stack. In output pixels.
Plain reading1.2 px means a feature that should sit in one place is typically a pixel and a bit away from it in the other frames.
Why exactThe sample scenes store the transform that was applied to each frame, so this is arithmetic, not an opinion about the picture.
Matters becauseMisalignment reaches fusion as double edges and ghosting — which the fusion metrics then blame on the fusion method.
Target
sub-pixel = invisible>1 px = ghosting
Residual — worstworst px
lower betterpx
The same measurement, for the single frame that ended up furthest out. The mean's companion.
Why bothA stack of forty frames, thirty-nine perfect and one badly misaligned, has an excellent mean. The output still has one ghosted layer running through it.
Goes upFeature matching failed on one frame — usually the ones furthest from the reference, where the least is in focus and there is least to match on.
Read asA wide gap between mean and worst points at a specific frame to look at, not at the pipeline as a whole.
Diagnosis
worst ≈ mean = uniformworst ≫ mean = one bad frame
Gainimprovement factor
higher better×
The misalignment before registration divided by the misalignment after. 4× means the pipeline removed three quarters of the error.
Why a ratioRaw pixel error depends on how badly the stack was shot. A ratio compares pipelines across scenes that were never equally shaky.
Below 1.0×Registration made the alignment worse than leaving the frames alone. The benchmark flags this explicitly — it is a real outcome, not a hypothetical.
DiminishingEach added stage costs time; gain is what says whether the stage paid for itself.
Scale
<1× harmful1× no effecthigher = better
Keptsurviving frame area
higher better%
How much of the original frame is left after cropping to the region every aligned frame covers. The price of alignment, in field of view.
Why it dropsShifting frames to line them up leaves each one missing a strip at some edge. Only the area they all cover can be kept.
The tradeA more aggressive pipeline can align better and crop harder. 98 % kept at 0.6 px error may beat 91 % at 0.4 px, depending on how tightly the shot was framed.
Not qualityIt measures cost, not correctness — but it is the number a photographer notices first.
Typical
85 %~97 % typical100 %
Selection sharevs. focus_index truth
closer to truth is better%
After registration, what share of the picture does the focus measure decide to take from the reference frame — and how does that compare with the share genuinely sharpest there?
What it testsWhether alignment error is pushing the focus decision onto the wrong frame. That is a registration failure that looks exactly like a fusion failure.
Reported asA ratio against the ground-truth share. 1.0× means the measure is choosing frames as often as it should.
Above 1The reference frame is winning territory it does not deserve — a classic sign that the other frames are blurred by misalignment.
Scale
<1× under-picked1.0× correct>1× over-picked
Detection widtha setting, not a score
tuning parameterpx
The width the frames are scaled to while the transform is being worked out. The full-size frames are then warped by the result — they are never upsampled, so nothing is thrown away.
Why it existsEstimating a transform on a 60 MP frame is slow. Estimating it on a 2000 px version is fast and, up to a point, just as accurate.
Too smallFine features vanish before matching sees them, and the residual rises.
Above frame widthMeans full-resolution detection: the setting is a cap, not a target.
Trade-off — residual vs. time
narrow = fast, imprecisewide = slow, precise

Never read one number alone

Every metric on this sheet can be gamed by a defect. Spatial frequency and entropy both reward noise. SSIM rewards smoothing. Detail share rewards over-sharpening. A change is only an improvement when the metrics it should not have moved held still.

Compare within a scene, never across

Spatial frequency spans 2.4 to 90.7 across the test scenarios — a 37-fold range driven entirely by subject, not by method quality. Rank methods on one scene at a time. A number quoted without its scene means nothing.

Tolerance is not precision

The tolerance column is how far a value may drift before the regression test treats it as a real change. It absorbs ordinary run-to-run variation. A move inside tolerance is not a small improvement — it is no evidence at all.
