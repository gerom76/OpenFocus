01

DCT — block cosine-transform fusion

Haghighat, Aghagolzadeh & Seyedarabi, Multi-focus image fusion for visual sensor networks in DCT domain, Comput. Electr. Eng. 37(5), 2011 · fusion_methods/dct.py, GPU twin dct_torch.py fastest classical, until 1.17.1order-dependent on near-ties

The published rule

  • Cut the frame into 8×8 blocks, DCT each one.
  • Score a block by the energy of all its AC coefficients — equivalently, its plain pixel variance.
  • Per block, argmax over frames gives a winner index map.
  • Consistency verification: majority/median filter over the index map.
  • Copy each winning block's pixels through verbatim.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Band-limit the measure. Subtract a box blur one block wide, then take the block mean of the squared detail. Contrast ≠ sharpness: a defocused highlight lays a smooth ramp that out-scores genuine fine texture, so the blurred frame won whole regions. wrong-frame background 35.7% → 2.0%
  • 2 · Pool over 3×3 blocks (_POOL_WINDOW) before comparing, so grain cannot flip a block.
  • 3 · Score each frame against its own noise — divide by the 10th percentile of that frame's block energies (_NOISE_PERCENTILE). Photon noise grows with brightness, so a bright defocused veil carried 4–12× the high-pass energy of a dark sharp frame in grain alone and stamped its tone across every featureless block.
  • 4 · No single winner. Every frame within _PLATEAU = 0.80 of the peak is the block's depth of field; the middle of that set is the focal plane, to sub-frame accuracy. Out of focus the set is the whole stack and the answer is mid-stack everywhere — uniform, not patchwork. on a 274-frame stack the old margin test abstained on 91% of blocks
  • 5 · Composite weights, not labels. Frame weights are upsampled across the block lattice, so the transition ramps instead of stepping. Output is no longer verbatim — every pixel stays inside its sources' envelope.
  • 6 · uint16 index map end to end — 256+ frame stacks used to crash or wrap.

Still open

  • Order dependence is reduced, not gone. Shuffling a 12-frame stack agrees to 33.6 dB (was 28.1). What remains is the near-tie that clears the margin. Any fix must land in dct.py and dct_torch.py in one commit or test_gpu_matches_cpu goes red.
  • CPU and GPU disagree on the noise floor — torch.quantile vs np.percentile flips a handful of near-tied blocks (41.5 dB on the veil fixture). Harmless today, a trap tomorrow.
  • Surface the confidence. best_energy − second_energy is already computed per block and thrown away; it is the one method where "how sure was it here" needs no new work.
  • Speed regressed by design — 274 frames of 2048×1364 now take 14.4 s against 5.3 s. Worth revisiting the two measurement passes.
  • Internal by choice: _POOL_WINDOW, _NOISE_PERCENTILE, _HIGHPASS_SCALE — load-bearing, and no user meaning.
02

Pyramid — Laplacian multiscale fusion

Burt & Adelson, The Laplacian pyramid as a compact image code, IEEE Trans. Comm. 31(4), 1983, with the classic choose-max fusion rule · fusion_methods/pyramid.py, GPU twin pyramid_torch.py order-independentpaper rule still selectable

The published rule

  • Build a Laplacian pyramid per frame.
  • For every band, copy the coefficient with the most local energy and discard the rest — choose-max.
  • Collapse the fused pyramid back to an image.
  • Coarse base band: plain mean over the stack.

Choose-max is right where one frame is plainly sharper — and wrong everywhere else, which on a macro frame is most of the picture.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Weight, don't choose. Each frame contributes (e / e_best) ** selectivity, default 8. A frame 2× behind the winner contributes 0.4%, so a real decision stays a decision; where frames tie they average — the correct answer for "nothing is in focus here". selectivity = inf reproduces the paper exactly.
  • 2 · Compare in units of the frame's own grain. Each band divided by a low percentile of its own energies, with exact zeros excluded so a clipped-black backdrop cannot read as noiseless. veil fixture: 32.3% → 0.0% of a smooth body given to a haze frame
  • 3 · Clamp to the source envelope. A collapsed pyramid sums bands from different frames and can land outside the stack's own range — measured 24 levels below the darkest source, which is what the reported dark filaments over a smooth corner were. invented pixels gone on all 8 fixtures
  • 4 · Let the detail carry the base. Base band weighted by aggregate activity at exponent 3, not a mean — 24 frames of haze used to outvote the few that resolved the subject. 18.5 → 35.1 dB
  • 5 · Streaming weights. The running peak is rescaled as frames arrive, exactly, so memory is bounded by the accumulators regardless of stack depth. All 8 scenarios improved; the two carrying the artefacts by 4.5 and 16.7 dB.

Still open

  • Scale coherence earns nothing yet. Shipped, default off: on every current fixture the envelope clamp already prevents what it was for, and it costs 2.4 dB on fine_texture, 5.3 on long_stack. Either find the stack that needs it or retire the dial.
  • Cost. 24 frames of 2048×1364: 3.6 s against 1.6 s for the old masked copy (selectivity=inf still runs at 1.4 s).
  • One metric moved the wrong way — defocus_seam_visible on deep_stack 1.5% → 2.7%, because the background got smooth enough to halve the local step it is measured against. Severity fell (0.466 → 0.257). The metric needs a floor.
  • No confidence output, and the private folder loader is one of the four copies of item 13.

03

Guided Filter (GFF)

Li, Kang & Hu, Image fusion with guided filtering, IEEE Trans. Image Process. 22(7), 2013 · fusion_methods/gff.py, GPU twin gff_torch.py bit-reproducibleclosest to its paper

The published rule

  • Two-scale decomposition: base = 31×31 mean filter, detail = image − base.
  • Saliency = Gaussian-blurred |Laplacian| (σr = 5); the per-pixel argmax gives a binary weight map per frame.
  • Refine each map with a guided filter, twice with different settings: base (r₁ = 45, ε₁ = 0.3), detail (r₂ = 7, ε₂ = 10⁻⁶).
  • Normalise the weights, fuse base and detail separately, add.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · r₁ is derived, not pinned. base_weight_radius() keeps the paper's 3:1 ratio to the averaging radius, so kernel_size = 31 still yields exactly r₁ = 45 — but small kernels no longer degrade the result (42.7 → 43.4 dB at kernel 7). Sweep spread across the whole slider fell from 0.74 dB to 0.03.
  • 2 · Nothing holds the whole stack. Frames are converted to float32 on demand, saliency is reduced with a running maximum, and a sliding window keeps exactly max_workers tasks in flight. 24 frames at 1536²: peak 5570 → 1704 MiB, flat in stack depth
  • 3 · Deterministic accumulation. Results are consumed in index order, so repeat renders are bit-identical; strict > reproduces argmax's lowest-index tie-break exactly.
  • Cost of 2 and 3: ~5% throughput, from capping the pool at 8 — the OpenCV filters are already internally parallel.

Still open

  • ε₂ is 10× the paper's. The code sets DEFAULT_EPS2 = 10e-6, which is 1×10⁻⁵, where Li et al. specify 10⁻⁶ — the shape of a transcription slip inherited from the reference implementation. Worth measuring both and either correcting it or writing down why it stays.
  • The kernel slider is inert by construction. GFF reconstructs base + detail exactly, so base layers reach the output only through the gap between the two weight maps, and that gap is small: every setting from 7 to 95 is visually identical. Investigated and closed — but the control is still on screen for a method it cannot change. Hide it here, or give it something to do.
  • Folder loader infers one extension from the first directory entry and globs only that, so a mixed-extension folder silently loses files (item 13; affects direct API callers, not the GUI).
  • No confidence output — the weight maps exist and are discarded.
04

DTCWT — dual-tree complex wavelet

Lewis, O'Callaghan, Nikolov et al., Pixel- and region-based image fusion with complex wavelets, Information Fusion 8(2), 2007 · fusion_methods/dtcwt.py, GPU twin dtcwt_torch.py order-independent since 1.30.13CPU cost superlinear

The published rule

  • Decompose each frame with the DTCWT: N levels, 6 orientations, complex coefficients.
  • Activity = coefficient magnitude, max-filtered over a small window.
  • Choose-max across all frames per coefficient, then a majority (consistency) filter over the decision mask.
  • Lowpass band: average. Inverse transform.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · One decision mask for all three channels. The reference implementation fused R, G and B in independent passes, so at a depth edge a pixel's red could come from one frame and its blue from another — a colour present in no source. The mask is now built from the strongest channel response and selects all channels together.
  • 2 · Activity-weighted lowpass. The plain mean ghosts large-scale disagreement (exposure drift, focus breathing) into the coarse band; each frame's lowpass is now weighted by its aggregate highpass activity, with an epsilon so a detail-free stack degrades gracefully to the old mean. mean abs. error 12.9 → 3.9–5.5 on the drift fixture
  • 3 · scipy out, OpenCV in. cv2.dilate and cv2.boxFilter(normalize=False) replace maximum_filter and convolve — verified bit-identical, and −24.5% CPU on a 6-frame 768² stack. Removed the last direct scipy import in the codebase.
  • 4 · Streaming fold bounds memory at roughly two frames of coefficients; the reconstruction is cropped back to the input geometry, which dtcwt's odd-size padding otherwise grows by a pixel.
  • 5 · One decision over the whole stack (1.30.13). The frames were folded together two at a time, so the early ones passed through more rounds of the consistency filter than the late ones and the picture depended on the order. Each coefficient now goes to the frame with the most activity around it, in one pass; the majority vote is replaced by pooling that activity over the window it used to count over, which is what the pyramid and DCT already do. order agreement 42–54 dB → byte-identical on 7 of 9 fixtures long_stack 32.65 → 35.46 dB 6–15% faster

Still open

  • CPU cost grows faster than area — 5.5× then 5.3× per quadrupling at 6 frames. At 2048² the CPU path takes tens of seconds against the GPU's fraction of one. The pairwise fold was the suspect and is now gone, and the growth did not move: it is the transform and its memory traffic.
  • Residual ties. Where two frames agree on the pooled activity and on the coefficient's own magnitude and differ only in phase, the earlier frame still wins — 334 pixels of long_stack, one of depth_edge. Anything further would be an arbitrary rule about complex numbers rather than a statement about focus.
  • Folder loader: the type-unstable sort key crashes on a folder mixing img1.png with cover.png (item 13).

GFG-FGF — gradient focus + fast guided filter

Fu Hongyu, Gong Yan, Wang Luhan et al., Multi-focus microscopy image fusion algorithm, Laser & Optoelectronics Progress 61(6), 2024 · fusion_methods/gfg_fgf.py, GPU twin gfg_fgf_torch.py not bit-reproduciblewhole stack in float32

The published rule / the code as inherited

  • Pick a working channel, measure a Scharr gradient focus score per frame on it.
  • Drop frames scoring below a global sharpness quota (15% of the best).
  • Local contrast (image − box blur), thresholded, then guided-filtered into an activity map per frame.
  • Per-pixel argmax → binary masks → guided-filtered again into smooth weights → weighted sum.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Split the two roles the single channel played. The guided filter's guide is luminance, as every other method uses; activity is measured on all three channels keeping the strongest response per pixel. A structure living in one channel is seen at full strength instead of being diluted by two flat channels. chromatic fixture: 25.8 → 40.5 dB, i.e. from "no better than not fusing" to fused
  • 2 · The 15% quota is gone. A frame can be globally soft and still the sharpest thing anywhere in a corner — on depth_edge the background frame scored 12% and the output was one source frame returned byte-for-byte, with no fusion at all. A frame is now dropped only when it carries no gradient energy whatsoever (> 1e-6 × max). +4.07 dB
  • 3 · Fast guided filter fallback (He et al. 2015, s = 4) when opencv-contrib's ximgproc is absent, so the method runs on a stock OpenCV install.
  • Cost of 1 and 2: 0.011 s → 0.015 s on a 320² three-frame stack. GPU twin agrees to 0.06 levels.

Still open

  • Accumulation runs in as_completed order, so the float summation order varies and two identical renders differ by about a level. GFF had exactly this defect and fixed it with _map_in_order; reusing that helper here is a small, self-contained change.
  • The whole stack is held as float32 BGR (imgs_f32, plus a full set of greys) — 12 bytes per pixel per frame, the same 2.9 GB-for-ten-24MP-frames problem GFF was cured of by lazy per-frame conversion. This is now the memory-hungriest classical method.
  • Three magic numbers with no provenance — g_gsz = 5, g_eps = 0.3, threshold = 0.005 — none swept, none exposed, none explained. The kernel slider (default 7) is the only control.
  • No confidence output.
06

Depth Map — hard select or contrast-weighted average

The DMap family: Zerene DMap, Helicon Methods A/B, Shine Stacker DepthMapStack — no single paper · fusion_methods/depthmap.py order-independent√N SNR in flat areasCPU only

The family's rule

  • Measure local contrast per pixel in the image plane, not in a transform domain.
  • Build a depth map — which frame is sharpest where — and smooth it.
  • Either hard-select the pixel from that frame (Zerene DMap, Helicon B) or average frames in proportion to contrast (Helicon A).
  • A "Radius" dial indirectly controls halo bleed.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Channel-tied energy. The Laplacian is taken per channel and the squared responses summed across channels first, so one grey activity map drives the decision and a pixel's colour can never split across sources — the failure DTCWT had for years.
  • 2 · A scale-free baseline in average mode. Every frame gets a floor weight of 10% of the stack's mean energy (absolute floor 1e-8). Where real focus energy is far above it, the sharp frame dominates; where it is at or below it, the weights go near-equal and the region collapses to the plain mean — which is what recovers the free √N noise reduction across the stack.
  • 3 · A dedicated halo-suppression radius (default 0, off). Grey-dilating each frame's pooled energy lets a sharply focused edge claim the band its own defocused glow contaminates in the other frames, so the ring renders as that frame's glow-free background. Competitors only offer this indirectly through their analysis radius — this is the one place the implementation is ahead of the field rather than level with it.
  • Ties are broken with strict > in index order, so both modes are bitwise-stable run to run.

Still open

  • The hard-select map is never regularised. MODE_MAX relies on box-filter pooling alone, where DCT median-filters its index map twice and DTCWT pools its activity over the decision window. Between depth planes the two pooled energies are near-equal, the winner alternates pixel to pixel, and the seam speckles. Every tool this method descends from smooths the depth map before gathering. An optional median or guided pass over the winning-index map closes it; MODE_AVERAGE needs nothing, blending is its own smoothing.
  • No GPU path. The only classical method without one, and the operations involved (Laplacian, box filter, dilate, argmax) are the easiest of the set to port.
  • The depth map is computed and discarded. This method literally builds the artefact four of five competitors export as a file — and the substrate for 3D/anaglyph output.
  • Folder loader: the third copy of item 13.

StackMFF-V4 — learned focus-index regression

Neural focus-index network, weights bundled in weights/ · fusion_methods/stackmffv4.py · torch, CUDA/MPS/CPU every output pixel is a source pixelno usable CPU path

The canonical form

  • Feed the network the whole grayscale stack as one tensor.
  • It regresses a per-pixel focus index — a continuous depth map over the stack.
  • Gather the fused image by indexing the colour stack with that map.
  • Encoder pools repeatedly, so input dimensions must be padded to a multiple of 32.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Depth-preserving gather. The grey input is normalised by each frame's own full scale, so the network sees [0,1] whether the stack is 8- or 16-bit; the colour gather then indexes the original array, so the stack's depth carries through untouched and no cast can truncate. Every output pixel is exactly some input pixel.
  • 2 · An explicit input floor. Below 112 px on the short edge the encoder collapses to a zero-sized feature map and torch raises with a tensor shape. The method now says so itself, and names the three ways out (output size, tile block size, another method).
  • 3 · Batched tiling with an adaptive cap. Tiles are padded to a common size and inferred as one batch; on CUDA OOM the batch cap halves and retries, so a long stack degrades in throughput instead of failing.

Still open

  • No CPU story. Usable but slow, and there is no smaller variant. Not a defect, but it shapes what can be recommended to a user without a GPU — and it is the one method where the fallback is "pick something else".
  • It loses its colour lead on deep stacks. StackMFF-V4 holds the best colour fidelity of the field almost everywhere, then posts 18.5 levels of deviation — the worst of the field — on the 64-frame deep_stack scenario. Never investigated.
  • Tile seams are untested at depth. The index map is resized with INTER_NEAREST and tiles are inferred independently, so nothing forces two neighbouring tiles onto a consistent depth.
  • No confidence output — the network's index map is exactly the depth map §6 wants exported.
08

IFCNN — refinement stage, not a fusion method

Runs after a fusion method on its all-in-focus candidate · fusion_methods/ifcnn.py · weights not bundled: drop the official checkpoint at weights/ifcnn.pth idempotent on its own outputoff unless weights are supplied

The canonical form

  • Encode the candidate and every aligned source frame into feature space.
  • Merge element-wise, so detail the fusion step missed can be recovered from whichever frame holds it.
  • Decode the merged features and output that image.

The round trip is lossy, and the loss lands on the whole frame as colour drift — measured at ~6× the guided filter's colour deviation, and on one scenario the refined picture scored below keeping the sharpest single frame.

What OpenFocus does instead — and why

  • 1 · Apply only the residual. The round-trip error is measurable in isolation — decode the candidate's own features with nothing merged in — so the stage returns
    candidate + (decode(merged) − decode(candidate)).
    The bracket is what merging contributed; the drift cancels. Refine an image against itself and the output is now bit-identical to the input, instead of paying a whole-frame round trip for nothing. 30.1 → 37.1 dB · edge 29.2 → 33.4 dB · colour error 6.89 → 2.87
  • 2 · Round, don't truncate. Both float→uint8 casts now use np.rint; truncation was removing half a level from every pixel, systematically. The audit that followed found five more of the same cast across fusion_methods/ and core/ and fixed all of them. DTCWT +2.41 dB average, +13.5 on low_contrast
  • Cost: one extra decode per block (conv3+conv4 on an already-computed feature map). Encoding, which dominates, is unchanged. An edge-gated variant was measured and rejected — better whole-frame numbers, 1.3 dB worse at boundaries, which is the one thing the stage exists to improve.

Still open

  • The weights are not bundled, so for most users the stage simply does not exist. Either ship them, or make the UI say plainly what is missing and where to get it.
  • The benefit is still unproven on real stacks. It now improves boundary rendering on 6 of 6 synthetic scenarios but costs overall PSNR on 3 of 6 — those fixtures hand it a near-perfect fusion and no genuinely missed detail, so they show its costs and not its benefits. The measurement that would settle it is a real stack, and it has not been made.
  • No control over strength. The residual is applied at 1.0 everywhere; a user-facing amount would let the trade be made per image rather than per release.

Synthesis

What to fix next — and why it isn't another fusion method

Impact × effort over every open item, per-method and cross-cutting

Open work, placed

Placement is a judgement from the measured audit, not a measurement. Two points are differentiators — graph-cut label optimisation and non-rigid alignment have no precedent in any of the five tools OpenFocus was compared against; everything else is catch-up or parity.

Cross-cutting, in priority order

#ItemWhy it outranks another fusion method
1Photometric alignment
catch-up		 Every focus measure in the codebase is contrast-linear — Scharr, Laplacian, DCT band energy, pooled Laplacian — so a 5% brighter frame wins the argmax on identical detail. It corrupts the input to all eight methods at once. Four of five competitors normalise brightness; three by default. OpenFocus does none of it.
2Surface the confidence
parity+		 Every method builds a decision map and throws it away. "The stack has no sharp frame here" is the most common cause of a disappointing render and is currently invisible. DCT already computes best − second per block.
3Export the depth map
catch-up		 1.8.0 turned the internal map into a blending method and 1.14.0 added halo suppression, but the map still cannot be saved. Four of five competitors write it out; for PICOLAY it is the point of the software. It is also the substrate for 3D / anaglyph output.
4Contrast / trust threshold
catch-up		 Zerene's DMap marks regions with too little contrast to judge and stretches the estimate across them. OpenFocus takes an unconditional argmax everywhere — including sky and smooth surfaces where the measure is pure noise — and the median filter that follows can only make the speckle blockier. DCT and Pyramid now have per-method noise gates; nothing generalises them.
5One folder loader
robustness		 Four copy-pasted private loaders. Two defects, both measured: a type-unstable sort key that raises TypeError on mixed filenames, and single-extension globbing that silently drops files. GUI-safe, API-hostile.
6Depth-wise slabbing
catch-up		 Three of five tools subdivide the stack along depth, fuse each group, then fuse the results. OpenFocus's tiling subdivides in X/Y for memory — a different operation for a different purpose. Cheap given the existing worker infrastructure.
7Selectable focus operator
parity+		 Shine Stacker alone exposes it — five interchangeable energy measures. The right operator is subject-dependent and no single choice wins everywhere.
8Graph-cut labelling
differentiator		 All six tools in the field comparison regularise the decision map with local filters. A global MRF — data term from the focus measure, smoothness penalising label changes, down-weighted at gradients — has no precedent to catch up to.

The shape of the result. OpenFocus has more fusion algorithms than anyone in its comparison set and fewer supporting stages than anyone. The competitors converge on two blending methods each and spend the rest of their effort on the pipeline around them. Both mandatory families are present — pyramid since 1.7.0, depth map since 1.8.0 — so the highest-value work left is that pipeline, not a ninth method.
