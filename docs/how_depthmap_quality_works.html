What the method does, and where it can go wrong

Depth Map scores every frame with one local focus measure, then lets that measure decide. The two modes differ only in how they use it — so a defect in the measure reaches both, while a defect in the selection reaches only Max. That split is what the rest of this note is organised around.

1 · MEASURE
per frame, shared
Smooth the frame slightly for measurement only, take its Laplacian, square it, sum across colour channels, and pool the result over a window. One grey activity map per frame. Because the channels are summed before pooling, all three channels of a pixel stay tied to one frame — colour can never split across sources. Defects 1 and 3 live here, and both modes inherit them.
2 · DECIDE
the two modes part
Max argmax records which frame wins each pixel — an explicit depth map, order-independent. Average blend turns the measures into weights, with a small baseline weight added so a flat region collapses to the plain mean and recovers the stack's √N noise advantage. Defect 2 lives here, and it is Max-only — a blend has no winner to get wrong.
3 · GATHER
at the stack's own depth
Max takes each pixel whole from the frame its index names — no interpolation, no float round trip, so an 8-bit stack returns 8-bit and a 16-bit stack returns 16-bit. Average normalises its weighted sum. Since 1.34.0 the depth map is despeckled before this step, so the gather reads a coherent map rather than the raw argmax.

Why none of this had been noticed before

All three are losses rather than additions — nothing bright or obviously broken appears, so each reads as "the stack never had detail there" unless another fusion method is put beside it. Defect 1 also scales with the kernel dial, and the shipped default is 9, where the ring is only 4 px wide.

Defect 1 — the measure reaches across occlusions

A box window is edge-blind. It does not know that half of what it is averaging belongs to a different depth.

The mechanism, measured

One frame holds a sharply focused, high-contrast contour. Walk right, off that contour and onto background that belongs to a different slice, and ask what each pooling says this frame's focus energy is out there. The raw squared Laplacian answers honestly: zero. The kernel-wide box does not.

Reference stack, kernel 51, each curve normalised to its own peak. Half-max reach past the boundary: raw 0 px · kernel-wide 25 px · narrow 7 px · geometric mean 11 px — and 25 px is exactly kernel / 2, which is how far a box window can see.
pooled over the kernel (51 px) — leaks
pooled over the narrow window (17 px)
geometric mean of the two — what ships

What it costs you

Inside the leaked band the foreground's frame out-measures every other frame, so it wins the argmax over pixels that are background — and supplies its own, defocused, version of them. The result is a band of genuinely lost detail tracing every outline.

In Average mode the same leak biases the weights rather than the winner, so the band is softened instead of replaced — the same defect, less loudly.

Halo suppression made it worse, by design

The dial grey-dilates the already pooled energy, so the claimed band becomes kernel/2 + radius. It is a tool for a different problem — a bright glow — and it fills a ring with defocused pixels rather than recovering what is behind it.

The fix

Pool the same energy at two scales and require a frame to carry both:

wide = _pool(energy, window)        # the dial's region measure
near = _pool(energy, window // 3)   # local evidence; barely leaks
energy = sqrt(wide * near)          # geometric mean

Their ratio says how much of a frame's regional score really belongs to this pixel's own neighbourhood — about 1 where the detail genuinely is, far below 1 in a band the wide window merely reached into. Where the energy really is uniform the two poolings agree and the mean is the plain pooled energy, so flat regions decide exactly as they did.

The narrow window is a divisor of the kernel, not a fixed size, so the correction stays the same fraction of whatever the caller dials in.

Defect 3, which came out with it — a prefilter for the measurement only

A bare ksize=3 Laplacian is the most noise-sensitive high-pass there is, and on flat, low-signal regions it was ranking frames by their noise floor rather than their focus — which picks whichever frame is most veiled, and mottles. Each frame is now smoothed by σ = 0.6 before its Laplacian is taken. This is a measurement prefilter: output pixels are still gathered from the untouched frames, so it costs nothing in output resolution. 0.6 is where the ground-truth scenarios settle, and the taps are pinned rather than left to OpenCV's sigma-to-ksize rule, so both compute paths convolve with bit-for-bit the same kernel.

Defect 2 — the decision has no spatial prior

Max only. Where no frame is genuinely sharper than the others, an argmax still returns an answer — it just returns noise.

Two pixels, two very different questions

Focus measure against frame number, measured on deep_stack (64 frames). Left: a pixel the stack resolves — one frame stands 25× above the field, and the argmax means something. Right: a pixel out of focus in every frame — the winner stands 1.63× above the field, which is noise. That argmax landed on frame 0 by chance.

Why noise becomes a mosaic

Neighbouring pixels resolve their coin-flip differently and take frames from opposite ends of the stack. Those frames render that same background at visibly different blur and brightness, so every disagreement becomes a hard edge.

Region, kernel 9>3 slices>15
background no frame resolves21.9%13.7%

Share of pixels choosing a frame that far from their own neighbourhood's choice. At kernel 51 the confetti coarsens into blobs — 6.5% — but the seams get larger, not fewer.

The fix — despeckle the depth map

A depth map is piecewise-smooth: it varies continuously across a surface and steps only at an occlusion. So the incoherent pixels are outliers against their own neighbourhood — which is exactly what a median removes while leaving genuine steps standing.

best_index = _regularise_index(best_index)
# 3 × cv2.medianBlur(idx, 5), before any pixel is gathered

Where the measure has a real peak the argmax already agrees with its neighbours, so the median is a no-op there — sharp detail is not what pays for this.

Three narrow passes, not one wide median

Iterating a narrow median converges towards its root signal — it removes what is smaller than the window without eroding what is larger. One wide median rounds off real depth features of its own size too. Three 5×5 passes matched a single true 9×9 on incoherence (seam density 0.0274 against 0.0259) while keeping more detail on the in-focus crops. It is also the only affordable option: 5 is the widest window cv2.medianBlur takes for anything wider than uint8.

What the ground truth said

The reporting stack has no reference, so nothing was settled on it. Every parameter was chosen on scenes whose all-in-focus truth is known.

deep_stack — the scenario written for this exact failure

64 frames over a subject that does not fill the frame, with a background that is never in focus in any of them. Quoting its own docstring: "a tie broken badly shows up as steps between frames that look nothing alike."

The subject band is identical in all three renders. Everything that changed is background the stack never resolved — which is the point: the fixes are supposed to be invisible where the measure was already right.

long_stack at kernel 51 — the same 1 px diagonals the leak was averaging away.

Average never had the mosaic. A weighted blend has no winner to get wrong, and a region where every frame ties simply averages — which is the correct answer there, and the source of its √N noise advantage. So the despeckle does not apply to it, and the code does not run it.

What Average did share is the measure. Both defects that live there reached it — and the size of the repair tracks the leak exactly: +2.18 dB at kernel 51, where the leak is 25 px wide, and a wash at the default kernel 9, where it is 4 px and the prefilter's small cost on the quieter scenes cancels it out.

That is the mechanism predicting its own result, which is the strongest check available without a reference.

On a real scene the difference can be slight

On the reporting stack, Average at kernel 51 moved by a mean of 1.5 levels out of 255. Average blends where Max selects, so it was never tearing to begin with — the fixes sharpen its weighting rather than rescue it from a visible artifact.

Reading the metrics honestly

Two numbers moved the wrong way and one scenario genuinely regressed. All three are worth understanding rather than rounding off.

spatial_frequency fell 14.48 → 9.80

On deep_stack, a metric labelled higher is better dropped by a third. The reference settles it: the reference's own spatial frequency is 8.54. The old render scored 69% above the truth, because the metric counts gradient energy without asking whether the reference has any there — and tearing is gradient energy.

The background moved from 4.9× the truth down to 2.7×, while the subject moved −5%. PSNR rose 1.76 dB and SSIM 0.089 at the same time.

long_stack lost 0.78 dB at kernel 51

A real regression, so it was located rather than argued away. That scenario is 12 depth bands 27 px tall, so a 51 px pooling window is already wider than a band; the median shifts those boundaries a couple of pixels further.

Interiors come out very slightly better. It saturates at one pass, so fewer passes do not buy it back. block_speckle also rose 0.156 — one 8×8 cell out of 640, the metric's own quantum.

A latent NaN came out with it

The two pooled terms the geometric mean multiplies are non-negative in exact arithmetic — but not in float32. boxFilter accumulates a running sum, and over a window spanning a sharp edge the cancellation can leave a tiny negative behind. Unclamped, that reached sqrt as a NaN — and a NaN loses every > it is compared with, so the pixel silently kept whichever frame happened to be there rather than the one that won. Clamped on both the CPU and GPU paths.

Best of three. Three median passes over one index plane cost ~15 ms on 1.7 MP — inside the run-to-run noise. The measure fixes of 1.33.0 cost about 0.9 s over 1.32.0: one extra box filter and a separable 7-tap Gaussian per frame.

CPU and GPU stay in step

The GPU path accumulates pixels rather than an index map, so it now tracks the index too, runs the median on the host through the same cv2 call, and re-gathers only the pixels it moved — skipping whole chunks of frames no moved pixel names.

Max bitwise identical, kernels 9 / 31 / 51 16-bit + halo bitwise identical Average within 2 LSB
The Average departure is pre-existing and documented: float summation order differs when a chunk is reduced as a batch.

The dial you should now leave alone

Halo suppression stays at 0 unless a bright glow actually survives. The two-scale measure discounts a veil on its own merits — a glow is low-frequency, so it scores badly on the narrow window even where it scores well on the wide one. The dilation does not remove a ring so much as fill one with defocused pixels, whether or not there was a glow to fight.
