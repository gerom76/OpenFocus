# Sample focus stacks

Ten stacks with full ground truth, for tuning fusion parameters and for
training anything that predicts a depth or selection map. Six are procedural;
four are rendered from a photograph, one of those at full capture resolution.
Alongside them sits one stack that was actually shot, which has no ground truth
and is here for the things a rendered scene cannot show — see
[Captured, not rendered](#captured-not-rendered).

The ten are generated by [generate_samples.py](generate_samples.py) and are
deterministic — regenerating produces identical bytes unless the generator
itself changed.

```bash
python samples/generate_samples.py
```

## Why these and not the fixtures in tests/

The fixtures in [tests/synthetic_stack.py](../tests/synthetic_stack.py) build a
slice by blurring everything outside a mask. That is cheap, repeatable, and
right for a regression test, but it is not how a lens defocuses a scene: the
sharp region and the blurred region are composited from the *same* image, so
nothing ever spreads across the boundary between them.

These are built from depth instead. Each scene is a set of layers at known
distances; each frame picks a focus distance, and every layer is convolved with
the disc its circle of confusion would have at that distance *before* being
composited front to back.

The consequence is the reason to use them. A defocused foreground spreads over
what sits behind it and carries some of the background's colour with it — the
partial occlusion that produces halos in real stacks, and that mask-based
fixtures cannot produce at all. The blur kernel is an anti-aliased disc rather
than a Gaussian, so out-of-focus edges double up the way an iris makes them,
which is the case focus measures actually misread.

## Layout

```text
samples/
  manifest.json           every scene's metadata in one file
  flower01/Sharing.png    the source photograph for the flower01_* scenes
  <scene>/
    frames/frame_NN.png   the stack, ordered near focus -> far focus
    ground_truth/
      all_in_focus.png    what a perfect fusion reproduces
      depth_map.png       16-bit, normalised over scene.json's depth_range
      focus_index.png     index of the sharpest frame, per pixel
      focus_index_preview.png    the same, colour-mapped for eyeballing
    contact_sheet.jpg     the whole stack at a glance
    scene.json           focus distances, noise, drift, seeds
```

## The scenes

| Scene | Frames | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `macro_dome` | 16 | Domed subject on a receding substrate. Continuous depth everywhere, so no frame is sharp anywhere but a ring. **Start here.** |
| `tilted_print` | 14 | A printed card raked away from the lens: linear depth ramp, fine type, recorded at **16 bits**. Selection errors print as horizontal bands. |
| `circuit_steps` | 12 | Four flat planes with hard silhouettes. Every error lands on an occlusion edge, which is where halos and colour bleed show. |
| `fibre_thicket` | 18 | Thin strands spread through depth. Defocused strands go translucent and let the background through — partial occlusion, the hard case. |
| `pale_specimen` | 12 | Flat pale subject at high ISO. Grain rivals the real detail, so variance-based focus measures pick badly. |
| `handheld_drift` | 16 | `macro_dome` recaptured with focus breathing, drift, roll and exposure flicker. Fails without registration. |

These six are 640×480. Scene distances are in arbitrary units — only ratios
matter — and blur radius in pixels is `26.0 * |1/z - 1/focus|`, capped at 22 px.

### Rendered from a photograph

Built by [photo_stacks.py](photo_stacks.py) from
[flower01/Sharing.png](flower01/Sharing.png), a fuchsia on slate — itself a
stacked image, so it is sharp throughout and can serve as the all-in-focus
truth. Same renderer, same ground truth, but the texture is a real photograph:
pollen grains a few pixels across, translucent petal edges, grain that was in
the image before anything was added to it.

| Scene | Frames | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `flower01_subject` | 14 | Focus covers the flower and stops. The slate behind it is defocused in every frame and never resolves — the everyday macro case, where a method has to leave the background alone. |
| `flower01_full` | 20 | The same frame with focus racked through the slate as well, so everything is sharp somewhere. A long throw where neighbouring frames barely differ. |
| `flower01_handheld` | 14 | The subject stack off a rig that is not quite rigid: breathing, drift, roll, exposure flicker. |
| `flower01_subject_hires` | 14 | `flower01_subject` at the photograph's own 2560×1430. For what only shows at capture resolution: whether a fixed block or kernel size still spans the detail it was tuned for, what a method costs in time and memory on a real frame, whether tiling leaves seams a small image never showed. |

The first three are 1280×715, blur radius `52.0 * |1/z - 1/focus|` capped at
44 px — the same aperture as the synthetic scenes, scaled for the wider frame.
The hires scene is 2560×1430 with the aperture scaled again (`104.0 * …`,
capped at 88 px), so it is the same scene on four times the pixels rather than
a different one: a result at 1280 and one at 2560 differ only by resolution.
Nothing is interpolated up to reach it — 2560 is the source's own width.

Rebuild the photo scenes alone, at any size:

```bash
python samples/photo_stacks.py --only hires     # just the expensive one
python samples/photo_stacks.py --width 1920     # the whole set at another size
```

Either way `manifest.json` is updated in place, so a partial rebuild cannot
leave a scene that `load_stack` refuses to open.

**The depth is assigned, not measured.** A photograph does not carry a depth
map and nothing here can recover one, so the flower is segmented by saturation
and brightness, depth ramps along the axis from the style tip at the lower left
to the far sepal at the upper right, the stamens are picked out as the
structures a morphological opening removes and pushed in front of the bell they
radiate from, and the slate is a plane receding towards the top of the frame.
The rendering is then physically consistent with that depth, which is what
makes the labels exact — the images were produced *from* the labels. It does
not make the depth true to the flower that was photographed. Measuring
depth-estimation accuracy against these measures agreement with an assumption.

The slate behind the flower is inpainted before rendering. Without it a
defocused foreground would composite against a hole and ring the subject with a
dark halo in every frame, which is the opposite of what an occlusion edge does.

Focus distances are spaced evenly in *reciprocal* distance, not in distance.
Even steps in distance would crowd the far end of the stack and leave a gap at
the near end, because depth of field grows with distance; stepping in 1/z gives
every frame a comparable share of the scene, which is what a focus rail set up
properly does.

## Captured, not rendered

One stack is a photograph all the way down. Nothing about it is known and
everything about it is real: the lens's own defocus with its doubled
out-of-focus edges, the magnification creeping frame to frame as the rail
advances, sensor grain that was there before anything was added, and a subject
with genuine occlusion.

| Capture | Frames | Size | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `electronics_ant` | 333 | 1819×1212 | An ant on a circuit board over a USB connector, shot on a focus rail. Hard occlusion — legs and antennae standing clear of the board — against dark glossy plastic that no frame resolves. The pairing that produces halos at the edges and grain-driven speckle everywhere else. |

It is loaded through [captures.py](captures.py) rather than `load.py`, because it
has no `scene.json`, no depth map and no focus-index labels — it cannot have
them.

```python
from samples.captures import fit_render, load_capture, load_render

frames, meta = load_capture("electronics_ant", step=12)   # every 12th frame
fused = my_fusion(frames)
ours, theirs = fit_render(fused, load_render("electronics_ant", "HF-C-6"))
```

### What is under `rendered/`

Other programs' results on the same stack, one folder per program. Two programs,
because one would be indistinguishable from a house style.

| Folder | Renders | What they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Affinity/` | 1 | Affinity Photo, at the capture's full 6064×4040, 16-bit JPEG XL. |
| `HeliconFocus/` | 10 | Helicon Focus across all three of its methods, at the stack's own resolution. **A** is its weighted average, **B** its depth map, **C** its pyramid. A and B are named `HF-<method>-<radius>-<smoothing>`, and C, which has no radius, `HF-C-<smoothing>`. |
| `OpenFocus/` | 1 | This project's own output. A **baseline**, not a reference — see below. `captures.third_party_renders()` leaves it out. |

Method C at four smoothing settings is what makes the set worth more than a
single reference, because four settings of one algorithm **bracket** rather than
score. Helicon's C is a Laplacian pyramid — the same algorithm as
[fusion_methods/pyramid.py](../fusion_methods/pyramid.py) — so recovering less
local contrast than it does at its lightest smoothing means soft, more than at
its heaviest means crunchy, and anything between is inside the range a mature
implementation of the same algorithm considers reasonable.

### The OpenFocus render is reproducible, and that is its whole value

Agreeing with ourselves is evidence of nothing, so this one is never used to
judge quality. What it is good for is the question the others cannot answer:
*did a change move what we ship, and by how much.*

Every render this project writes carries its settings in an XMP box, which
`captures.read_render_options()` reads back — the shipped one records 333
frames, Scale then Homography registration off the first frame, the pyramid at
levels 5 / selectivity 8 / coherence 0.75 / base weighting 0, and auto contrast
at 50%. Running that recipe again reproduces the file to **0.4 of an 8-bit
level** (49.4 dB), landing on the same 949×1458 crop, so it is the one
comparison here where pixels *are* comparable — both sides come out of the same
pipeline instead of two programs that each aligned the stack their own way.

After a deliberate change to the method, re-render at the same settings and
replace the file.

### None of them is a ground truth

Not one of these renders can be compared pixel by pixel, and the reason is the
same for both programs: each aligned the stack before fusing it, and focus
breathing means the magnification each removed differs frame to frame. Their
output matches the geometry of no raw frame, and no single warp relates them
either — fitting one by ECC reaches a correlation of 0.45 to 0.50 and leaves a
residual shift of five to ten pixels. Affinity adds two obstacles of its own: it
renders at the capture's full size while the frames are a downscale, so it
resolves detail no frame in the stack contains, and it carries its own tone
curve at about half the mean level of the frames.

Read pixel by pixel every one of them ranks a *single defocused frame* about
five decibels above a correct fusion. What they do answer is the coarser
question — did a method find detail in the same places these programs found it,
and how much of it — which `fusion_metrics.detail_agreement` measures over tiles
wide enough to absorb the misalignment.
[tests/test_pyramid_electronics_ant.py](../tests/test_pyramid_electronics_ant.py)
is what that looks like in practice.

Unlike everything above, this cannot be regenerated: `generate_samples.py` does
not produce it and nothing else can. Callers that cannot find it should skip —
`captures.is_available()` is there for that.

## Using them

```python
from samples.load import load_stack
from tests.fusion_metrics import psnr, ssim
from tests.fusion_registry import get

stack, truth, meta = load_stack("circuit_steps")

method = get("dct")
name, values, _ = method.sweeps[0]
for value in values:
    fused = method.run(stack, **{name: value})
    print(name, value, psnr(fused, truth["all_in_focus"]))
```

`load_stack` returns the frames near-focus first, a ground-truth dict
(`all_in_focus`, `depth` in scene units, `focus_index`), and the scene metadata.
It rescales the 16-bit scene to 8 bits by default so metrics stay comparable
across scenes; pass `as_uint8=False` to keep the full depth.

That sweep spreads about 11 dB across `circuit_steps`, so the samples do
separate parameter choices rather than scoring everything the same. Fused PSNR
against the ground truth lands in the high 20s to high 30s for the current
methods — low enough to leave headroom, high enough that a regression is
visible.

For training a selection or depth map, `focus_index.png` is the per-pixel label
and `depth_map.png` the continuous target. Read the next section first.

## The test that runs on them

[tests/test_sample_stacks.py](../tests/test_sample_stacks.py) checks every CPU
method against this set — it skips itself with instructions if `samples/` has
not been generated. It asserts the things only depth-rendered ground truth can
support:

- fusion beats the best single frame by a clear margin on the four scenes where
  that is a fair demand, and does not fall *below* it on `pale_specimen`
- where the label says frame *k* is focused, the result resembles frame *k*
  more than a frame at least four away — checked only on textured pixels, and
  only on scenes whose recorded `focus_index_agreement` clears 0.85
- error along the exact depth discontinuities in `circuit_steps` stays bounded,
  both in absolute levels and relative to the error away from them
- fusing `tilted_print` at 16 bits lands within 0.5 dB of the 8-bit result
- registering `handheld_drift` raises `Q_ABF`, and un-warping it with
  the affines in `scene.json` recovers the quality the drift costs

Thresholds sit well clear of the measured values, and each was checked to
actually fail on a deliberately broken fusion rather than merely to pass on a
working one.

## What these samples are not

**The focus index is exact, but not always meaningful.** It is derived from the
depth map, so it is correct by construction — but only where "the depth at this
pixel" is a sensible thing to say. On a translucent strand, or on a flat patch
with nothing to resolve, one integer per pixel cannot describe what the stack
contains. `scene.json` records `focus_index_agreement`: the fraction of pixels
where a plain Laplacian focus measure picks the labelled frame or a neighbour.

| Scene | Agreement |
| --- | --- |
| `tilted_print` | 1.00 |
| `circuit_steps` | 0.91 |
| `macro_dome` | 0.85 |
| `handheld_drift` | 0.73 |
| `flower01_full` | 0.66 |
| `flower01_subject` | 0.48 |
| `pale_specimen` | 0.40 |
| `flower01_subject_hires` | 0.40 |
| `flower01_handheld` | 0.34 |
| `fibre_thicket` | 0.19 |

The low numbers are the scenes doing their job, not a defect: `pale_specimen`
and `fibre_thicket` exist precisely because a single label per pixel is the
wrong model there. Train a per-pixel classifier on those two and it will be
fitting noise. `handheld_drift` is low for a different reason — its ground truth
is in the *undrifted* geometry, so it only lines up after registration, which is
the point of the scene.

**The flower scenes need one more caveat.** The slate is 82% of the frame, and
in `flower01_subject` focus stops before reaching it, so every one of those
pixels is labelled frame 13 — the last one. That label is honest about what it
means (*the least defocused frame*) and useless as a training target (*nothing
is sharp here*). Mask by depth, or use `flower01_full`, where focus does run
through the slate and its labels span frames 13–18.

**Ground truth carries the same noise and vignetting as the frames**, so PSNR
against it is not capped by a mismatched noise floor — but it also means no
method can score infinitely well.

**The rendering is synthetic even where the texture is not.** There is no
chromatic aberration, no lens distortion, no diffraction, the aperture is a
clean disc rather than a real iris, and the added noise is a shot-plus-read
approximation rather than a sensor profile. The flower scenes borrow a real
photograph's detail, not a real lens's behaviour. They are for tuning and for
sanity-checking cases that are hard to capture reproducibly — not a substitute
for testing against real captures before a release.

alone, another 59 MB the three flower scenes at 1280×715, and 23 MB
